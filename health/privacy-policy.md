# Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

## The short version

- **Health Track works completely offline.** The app has no permission to use the internet, so it cannot send your data anywhere.
- **Everything you enter stays on your phone.** There is no account, no sign-in, no cloud sync, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
- **We never receive your health data.** We can't see it, sell it or share it, because it never reaches us.
- Your data leaves your phone only when **you** move it: a backup file you save, a report image you save, or an email you choose to send us.

## Who we are

Health Track is made by Rosi Tech Labs, an independent app developer ("we", "us"). You can reach us at [rositech104@gmail.com](mailto:rositech104@gmail.com).

## What the app keeps on your phone

To work, the app saves what you enter, in its own private storage on your phone:

- Your profile: name (optional), sex, birth year, height, weight and activity level, used to work out your calorie and nutrition goals.
- Your goals and how they changed over time.
- Food logs, recipes, saved meals and your own foods.
- Exercise logs, workouts and routines.
- Body measurements (weight, waist and others) and progress photos.
- Sleep, habits, reminders and your app settings.

Other apps can't read this storage, and Android encrypts it on the phone. It stays there until you delete it (see "Deleting your data").

## What we collect

**Nothing.** The app has no internet permission, so nothing you enter can be sent to us or to anyone else. Android's own cloud backup (Google backup) is also switched off for this app, so your data is not copied to Google's servers either.

## Features that use your camera or other apps

- **Label scanner (camera).** When you scan a nutrition label, the photo is read on your phone by Google's ML Kit text recognition, which is built into the app. The photo is deleted as soon as it has been read. Nothing is uploaded. ML Kit is designed to send usage and error statistics to Google, but in this app it can't: the app has no internet permission, and the part of ML Kit that would upload them has been removed.
- **Images you share into the app.** If you share a label photo or screenshot from another app, it is read the same way, on your phone.
- **Notifications.** Reminders are scheduled and shown by your phone. Nothing is sent from a server.
- **Home-screen widgets.** If you add a widget, it shows today's numbers (like calories) on your home screen, where anyone who sees your screen can see them.
- **Progress photos.** Saved privately inside the app, and included in a Full backup.

## Backups and exports (you are in control)

- **Full backup (JSON) and CSV exports** are saved where you choose. These files are **not encrypted**: anyone who gets the file can read it. Keep them somewhere private.
- **Auto-backup** saves the same kind of file to a folder you pick. If that folder is a cloud folder (for example Google Drive), the cloud service stores the file under its own privacy policy.
- **Report images** you save go to your phone's Pictures folder, where your gallery app, and any photo backup you use, can see them.

## Buying Pro

Pro is a one-time purchase made through Google Play.

- Google handles the payment. We never see your card, UPI or bank details. Google's own privacy policy applies to the payment.
- To know whether you own Pro, the app asks the Google Play Store app on your phone, and remembers the yes or no answer on your phone.
- Like every Play developer, we receive sales reports from Google (such as order number, date, product, price and country). We use them only for accounting, tax, refunds and support.

## Emails you send us

If you write to us, including with the app's **Send feedback** or **crash report** buttons, we receive your email address and what you write.

- **Send feedback** adds the app version, your Android version and your phone model, so we know what you are using.
- **A crash report** contains the time of the crash, those same phone details, and the technical place in the app's code where it failed. It never contains your logs, numbers, photos or anything else you entered. You see the full text before you send it, and it is only sent if you press Send in your email app.

We use emails only to reply to you and to fix problems. We delete them when they're no longer needed, or sooner if you ask.

## Crash statistics from Google Play

If the app crashes and you have allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics with Google, Android may report the crash to Google ("Android vitals"). This is Google's own system, covered by Google's privacy policy. It does not include the data you entered in the app. We see these reports in Google Play Console as technical error details and totals, which help us fix bugs.

## Deleting your data

- In the app: **More → Delete all data** removes everything the app keeps on your phone.
- Uninstalling the app also deletes it.
- Backup files, CSV files and report images you saved outside the app are yours. Delete them from where you saved them.

Because we never receive your app data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side, except emails you have sent us. Ask and we will delete them.

## Children

Health Track is meant for adults (18 and over). It is not designed for children.

## Your rights

You can ask us any question about your privacy, or raise a complaint, at [rositech104@gmail.com](mailto:rositech104@gmail.com). We aim to reply within 7 days. Since we hold no data about you apart from emails you send us, requests to see, correct or delete your data are about those emails. Everything else is on your phone, under your control.

## Security

Your data stays in the app's private storage, which Android encrypts. We recommend a screen lock on your phone. Backup and CSV files are not encrypted, so store them somewhere private.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the date at the top, update the copy inside the app, and mention important changes in the app's release notes. We will never start collecting your data without changing the app, this policy and Google Play's data safety section first.
